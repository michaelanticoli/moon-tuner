Five stations. The shortest rite in the series, because sealing is a single decisive act that doesn't improve with deliberation. You identify, diagnose, design, test, and cure. Then it's done.

A boundary is not a feeling. It is not a wish, a resolution, or a statement of intent. A boundary is a structure: a specific condition, a specific consequence, and a specific action you take when the condition is met. If any of those three pieces is missing, it's not a boundary. It's a hope. Hopes don't hold.

The aim is to build one boundary that actually works — tested, held, cured — and to understand why the previous ones didn't.

The seal is set. Not because you decided, but because you tested and it held.

Boundaries built on declaration dissolve. Boundaries built on architecture — condition, consequence, action — hold because they don't depend on your mood, your courage, or the other person's compliance. They hold because the structure holds.

Sealed, not just stated.
Tested, not just intended.
Held, not just hoped.

The seal is curing. Use it again. Each repetition makes it structural.
