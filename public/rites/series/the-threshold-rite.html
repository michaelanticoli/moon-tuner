Seven stations for a rite about beginning — because the irony of never starting is that the analysis phase has no natural end. This one gives it one.

You are not lazy. Lazy people don't agonize. They shrug and move on. You stand at the threshold and think — about readiness, about the right moment, about what could go wrong, about needing just a little more information before you can responsibly commit. The thinking feels like preparation. It is not. It is the avoidance.

The aim is one crossed threshold. Not the right one, not the perfect one. One that's been open long enough for the standing-in-it to cost more than the walking-through.

The threshold is behind you. Not because the moment was perfect, but because you stopped waiting for a moment that would never come and walked through the one that was actually there.

Readiness is not a feeling. It's a story you tell yourself while the door stays open and the opportunity gets smaller. You were ready before this rite. You just hadn't moved yet.

Begun, not ready.
Moving, not waiting.
Through the door, not studying it.

The threshold is crossed. The beginning already happened. Keep moving.
